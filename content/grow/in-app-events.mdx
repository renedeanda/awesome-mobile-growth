## What an in-app event gets you

An in-app event is a timely thing happening inside your app: a challenge, a premiere, a new season, a major update. Apple shows it as a card on your product page and in search results, and can pick it for editorial and personalized spots on the Today, Games and Apps tabs. People can also ask the App Store to notify them when it starts.

That makes events one of the few ways to get new store placement without a new version. If your app is already approved, you can submit an event with or without a new app version.

Events show on iOS 15, iPadOS 15 and later.

## The fields and their limits

<MetadataLimits group="events"/>

Apple's [in-app events guidance](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/in-app-events/) asks for the event name in title case and the descriptions in sentence case. Don't name the event type in the title ("Weekend Challenge") because the badge already shows it. Apple says specific prices in event metadata lead to rejection.

<Example title="A film photo log app">
<p><strong>Badge:</strong> Challenge</p>
<p><strong>Event name:</strong> Golden Hour Week</p>
<p><strong>Short description:</strong> Shoot one roll at sunset, log every frame</p>
<p><strong>Long description:</strong> Log a full roll shot in the hour before sunset and compare exposures with other film shooters.</p>
</Example>

Every event also has a deep link (a universal link is Apple's recommendation; avoid URL shorteners), a priority (High Priority events appear ahead of Normal ones), an event purpose and a setting for whether an in-app purchase is required.

Draft all four fields against these limits in the [Workbench In-app event tab](/workbench/?tab=event).

## Badges

Pick the badge that matches what's happening: **Live Event**, **Premiere**, **Challenge**, **Competition**, **New Season**, **Major Update** or **Special Event**.

## Media

<AppleEventMediaTable/>

Apple's guidance for the media: videos autoplay and repeat, so make them loop smoothly, and don't add borders or gradients.

## Timing rules

| Rule | Limit |
| --- | --- |
| Event length | At least 15 minutes, at most 31 days |
| Publish before start | Up to 14 days before the start date |
| Different start times by region | All start dates within 48 hours of each other |
| Published at once | Up to 10 events on the App Store and Apple Games |
| Approved per app | Up to 15 in App Store Connect |

The publish date is when the event card can appear before the event starts. Use those days: people who tap "Notify me" during that window hear from the App Store when it begins.

## Measure it

App Store Connect reports event impressions, event details page views, app opens from the event and how many people asked to be notified. You can also filter downloads and acquisition sources by event.

Compare the downloads during an event with the same days in the weeks before it. A spike in event impressions with flat downloads means the card was seen but didn't persuade; change the media or the short description for the next one.

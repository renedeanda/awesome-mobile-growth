## Treat the sequence as an argument

Each frame should earn the next one. A useful sequence often moves from core outcome, to recognizable situation, to differentiating proof, to trust or depth. That is a starting hypothesis—not a universal template.

## Give each frame one job

Write a screenshot brief with four columns: frame, single message, product evidence, and asset status. If a frame needs two headlines to explain itself, split the idea or choose the stronger claim.

## Design for the scan

Test the first frames at thumbnail size and on the physical devices your audience uses. Preserve contrast, avoid tiny interface chrome, and do not depend on decorative mockups to carry meaning.

## Show what exists

Use real, current UI wherever practical. Seed representative demo data without exposing private information. If imagery is illustrative, keep it clearly separate from product capability claims.

## Connect creative to retention

A high-converting screenshot can still be wrong if it attracts people expecting a product you did not build. Read conversion with activation, retention, refunds, ratings, and support themes.

### Practice

Draft the sequence in the workbench, then capture real product states. Validate exact current asset specifications in App Store Connect before export.
